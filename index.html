A way of thinking

Think like an economist.

Economic thinking involves more than applying a model. It means recognising assumptions, tracing cause-and-effect relationships and testing explanations against evidence.

  • Identify: What economic issue or change is occurring?
  • Explain: Which economic relationships help explain it?
  • Apply: What does the evidence suggest in this context?
  • Evaluate: How effective, equitable or sustainable is the outcome?

Ask better questions

Use these prompts to turn a visual pattern into a more rigorous economic inquiry.

  • What is the overall direction of the data?
  • Which period shows a change in direction or pace?
  • What is the percentage change across the period?
  • Which demand-side or supply-side factors could explain the trend?
  • What additional evidence would help test your explanation?
Data discipline: Always check the indicator, units, time period, source and whether the data are nominal or real before drawing conclusions.

Learning pathway 01

Microeconomics: choices and markets.

Scarcity means choices must be made. Microeconomics examines how those choices interact through markets and how resources are allocated.

Scarcity and choice

Explore limited resources, unlimited wants, opportunity cost and the production possibility frontier.

Demand and supply

Analyse how changes in price and non-price determinants influence market outcomes.

Market failure

Investigate externalities, public goods, information asymmetry and policy responses.

Learning pathway 02

Macroeconomics: the whole economy.

Macroeconomics considers aggregate outcomes and the relationships between economic growth, price stability, employment and living standards.

Economic growth

Distinguish nominal and real GDP, examine productivity and consider the limits of GDP as a wellbeing measure.

Inflation and unemployment

Interpret changes in the price level and labour market conditions, including their causes and consequences.

Macroeconomic policy

Explore monetary and fiscal policy, transmission mechanisms, policy lags and trade-offs.

Learning pathway 03

International Economics: an interconnected world.

Countries are linked through trade, finance and investment. Examine the benefits, costs and distributional effects of global economic integration.

International trade

Use comparative advantage to explain specialisation and the gains from trade.

Protection and trade policy

Evaluate tariffs, quotas and subsidies, including impacts on consumers, producers and governments.

Exchange rates

Explore currency appreciation and depreciation and their effects on trade and the domestic economy.
